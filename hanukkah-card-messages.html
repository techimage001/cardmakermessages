Questions About Hanukkah Card Messages

Can I send a hanukkah card digitally?

Yes. Choose one of the digital sizes and export a PNG or JPEG, which sends cleanly through messaging apps without awkward cropping. Cards without a personal photograph can also be shared as a link, which shows the recipient a preview of the card along with a way to make their own. Cards that include your own photograph are sent as the image itself, because the photograph stays on your device rather than being uploaded anywhere. Everything on this hanukkah page works with the card maker in your browser, with no account, no watermarks on downloaded cards and no limit on how many cards you make.

How should I sign off a hanukkah card?

Match the sign off to the message. A warm message closes naturally with something like "with love" and your name; a plainer message reads better with "best wishes" or simply your name on its own. On the card itself the closing wish and your name are separate lines, each with its own font and size control, so you can keep the closing line small and let it sit quietly under the message rather than competing with it. Avoid stacking several closings together. On this page the hanukkah wording is grouped by tone, so comparing two versions side by side usually settles it faster than starting from a blank card.

Are these hanukkah messages yours to use?

Nothing is held back. You can use any of these hanukkah messages personally or commercially, and the finished card carries no branding of ours unless you add it. The messages are written specifically for this site, which matters more than it sounds: printed verses found elsewhere online are often under copyright, and putting one on a card you hand to somebody is exactly the situation where that becomes a problem. Everything here is safe to print. If you are still deciding, read the hanukkah examples in two different tones and see which one you would be comfortable reading aloud to the person.

How do I make a hanukkah card online?

Choose a design, set the font and size of each line if you want to, review the finished card, then download it. Exports include a print ready PDF and PNG or JPEG images. Everything runs in your browser, so there is no account and no upload.

What tone should a hanukkah card use?

Think about how you would speak to this person face to face, then write closer to that than to a greetings card cliche. For hanukkah, the tones available here are heartfelt, short, formal, religious, inspirational, which covers most relationships you are likely to be writing for. The safest default when you genuinely do not know is plain and short: it reads as sincere rather than distant, and it never strikes the wrong note the way forced cheerfulness can. Because these hanukkah lines are written for this site rather than gathered from elsewhere, you can print any of them without worrying about who wrote them first.

How long should a hanukkah message be?

One to three sentences suits most hanukkah cards. Shorter works better when the relationship is formal or the subject is delicate, because restraint reads as respect. Longer suits someone close who will read the card more than once. The format matters too: a folded card gives you an inside panel with room to write properly, while a card you send digitally reads better short enough to take in at a glance. When you are unsure, write the shorter version and let the specific detail carry the weight.

Should I handwrite the hanukkah message?

Printing the message and adding a handwritten line underneath is a good middle ground, and it is what most people do with a printed card. The card maker has a handwritten style among the font choices if you would rather the whole thing looked informal. If you are printing to fold, leave the inside panel lighter than you think you need: handwriting takes more room than typed text, and a cramped inside panel undoes the work the front cover does. If you are still deciding, read the hanukkah examples in two different tones and see which one you would be comfortable reading aloud to the person.
