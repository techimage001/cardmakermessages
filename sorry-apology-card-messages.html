How should I sign off a sorry and apology card?

Match the sign off to the message. A warm message closes naturally with something like "with love" and your name; a plainer message reads better with "best wishes" or simply your name on its own. On the card itself the closing wish and your name are separate lines, each with its own font and size control, so you can keep the closing line small and let it sit quietly under the message rather than competing with it. Avoid stacking several closings together. What tone should a sorry and apology card use?

Match the tone to the relationship rather than the occasion. This page covers heartfelt, short, formal, inspirational wording so the choice is deliberate rather than accidental. Warmer, more personal language suits family and close friends. Plainer, more measured language suits colleagues, clients and people you know less well. You can change tone at any point in the card maker without losing the design work you have already done. How long should a sorry and apology message be?

There is no fixed length, but sorry and apology messages rarely benefit from going past four sentences. Length is not what makes a card feel meant. Specificity is. A single line naming something real will outlast three paragraphs of general good wishes. If you have chosen a folded card you have an inside panel to fill, so a slightly fuller message sits comfortably there; on a one page or digital card, tighten it.
