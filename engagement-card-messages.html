Can I print a engagement card at home?

Home printing works well if you get two settings right. Print at actual size rather than letting the printer scale the page, and when printing both sides choose landscape with short edge flipping so the inside lands the right way up. The exported PDF already has the fold line and panel positions worked out. Card stock behaves differently from paper in most home printers, so it is worth one test sheet before you commit the good stock. What is a short engagement message?

Something like "Congratulations on your engagement." does the job when space is tight or the relationship is more formal. Short messages are not a lesser option: on a engagement card they often read as more sincere than a long one, because there is nowhere to hide behind phrasing. The rule that helps is to cut anything that would be true of anybody, and keep only what is true of this person. This page has several short options grouped together so you can compare them.

What do you write in a engagement card?

Start with the relationship, not the occasion. A engagement card for someone close can carry detail and warmth; one for a colleague or acquaintance works better kept plain and short. Say the true thing first and stop there. Every example here was written for engagement specifically rather than adapted from a generic template, which is why the wording sits differently from an all purpose message. Copy any line as it stands, or treat it as a first draft and rewrite it in your own words.

How long should a engagement message be?

One to three sentences suits most engagement cards. Shorter works better when the relationship is formal or the subject is delicate, because restraint reads as respect. Longer suits someone close who will read the card more than once. The format matters too: a folded card gives you an inside panel with room to write properly, while a card you send digitally reads better short enough to take in at a glance. When you are unsure, write the shorter version and let the specific detail carry the weight.
